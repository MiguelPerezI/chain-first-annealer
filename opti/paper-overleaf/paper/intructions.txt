What arXiv moderation actually does is screen for category, format, and obvious spam/plagiarism — not correctness. Actual verification happens entirely outside arXiv, through two separate tracks:

- **Formal**: submission to a journal → 2–3 reviewers check it → if accepted, the arXiv entry gets a "Journal reference" / DOI field added pointing to the published version. That's the closest thing to a discrete verification event, but it means a handful of reviewers signed off — not that the field has reached consensus. Published results get retracted after the fact too.
- **Informal**: the community itself checks it — other researchers cite it, build on it, or (for major claims) write independent expository papers walking through the argument. This is literally how Perelman's proof became accepted: no journal submission at all, just several groups independently writing up and confirming the argument over roughly two years before it was treated as settled.

So "getting verified" isn't a single event you can point to — it's an accumulation of independent people finding no hole in it over time. The risk in expecting a binary state: if you treat "not yet formally verified" as evidence something's wrong, you'll misread the normal, slow, unglamorous default that most correct results also sit in for years — reviewer bandwidth and researcher attention are scarce, not doubt about your specific result.
